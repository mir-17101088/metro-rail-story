Dhaka’s Metro Rail: Essential Transport, Escalating Costs

In 2005, the government and the World Bank formulated a 20-year Strategic Transport Plan that recommended mass rapid transit (MRT) corridors.

This plan was revised and approved by the government in 2016. Called the Revised Strategic Transport Plan (RSTP 2015-2035) establishes a comprehensive urban transit blueprint initially proposing five Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) lines and two Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) lines, later expanded by DMTCL to six MRT lines spanning over 100 stations.

The Executive Committee of the National Economic Council (ECNEC) formally approved the MRT Line-6 project with Japan's JICA in 2012.

The initial cost of the project was slated to be Tk 21,985.07 crore at inception. By the end, when the line was inaugurated in 2022, the costs had escalated to Tk 32,717.72 crore, a rise of 48.82 percent.

One station is yet to be added to the MRT 6 line. This station will be at the end of the line, in Kamlapur, and will be added by early 2027.

At the moment, nearly 80 percent (77.2%) of its construction is complete.

Line-1 and Line 5N were approved on October 15, 2019 for a combined cost of Tk 93,800 crore.

Line 1 has a 19.87 km underground section connecting Dhaka airport with Kamalapur, and an 11.37 km elevated section from Natunbazar to Pitalganj Depot in Narayanganj, while Line-5N runs from Hemayetpur to Bhatara.

On September 16, 2026, ECNEC approved the Line-1 and Line-5 (North) with a revised cost of Tk 2,04,242 crore. Metro Line-1 will cost Tk 1,14,394 crore while Line-5 (North) will cost Tk 89,848 crore.

Line-1 & 5N will be Bangladesh's first Underground Rail Projects. While Line-6 rides on elevated concrete piers above the city streets, 12 of the 21 stations of MRT Line-1, and 9 out of 14 stations of MRT Line-5 North will be subterranean. MRT Line-1 will be the country's first subway.

Elevated Underground

On the same day, ECNEC approved a third line, Line-5 (South), at a cost of Tk 45,504 crore.

The project will run from this month to August 2033 and will connect Gabtoli in the west to Dasherkandi in the east while passing through major central areas including Shyamoli, Asad Gate, Karwan Bazar, Hatirjheel, Tejgaon, and Aftabnagar.

More than two-thirds of the 17.2km line–about 12.8 km–will be underground.

The Dhaka Mass Transit Company Limited (DMTCL) is planning a fresh timeline for developing five additional metro rail corridors in the megacity, after a lapse of over a year, as the current target of completing all Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) lines by 2030 was never considered realistic.

Officials indicated that the earlier target of completing all MRT lines by 2030 is now unlikely to be met, with a revised completion date likely around 2035.

By the time of its completion, the MRT network is slated to serve millions of commuters, taking a huge strain off Dhaka's chronically traffic-logged streets. The MRT-6 already conducts 300,000 trips per day, while the combined total of MRT lines 5 Northern and Southern is forecast to 1,339,197 daily boardings.

But this came at a cost, and the cost was way more than was initially planned.
